# Project: MIDI Companion (TCSS 490 Artifact 2)

## Goal
A browser MIDI player and Synthesia-style visualizer: load a `.mid` file, watch notes fall onto a piano, and play along on my own MIDI keyboard. Learn mode (play along with the file) matters most. Small scope, built in the week the artifact allows (due Sun Oct 18).

## Stack
- Vite + TypeScript, no UI framework, Canvas 2D for drawing.
- Hosted on Vercel at `midi-companion.vercel.app`. Every push to `main` redeploys.
- Reference app: midee (`github.com/aayushdutt/midee`). It has no license, so ideas only. Do not copy its code, assets, or styling.

## Code map
- `src/clock.ts`: the one clock. It is given the audio clock (`Tone.immediate()`) by `main.ts`, so the picture and the sound share it, and it has no Tone import so `scripts/test-clock.mjs` can test it with a fake clock. Holds still for a short head start after Play or a seek. It has a playback speed (`setSpeed`); song time moves at that rate, and `audioTimeAt` accounts for it.
- `src/sustain.ts`: the sustain pedal logic for notes I play by hand (no browser code). While the pedal is down, released keys keep ringing, and pedal up lets them all go, except keys still held. Tested by `scripts/test-sustain.mjs`.
- `src/piano.ts`: loads the Salamander samples once. They are copies kept in `public/samples/salamander/` (30 mp3 files, 1.92 MB), and `vercel.json` gives that folder a one-year cache time so a visitor's browser keeps them. Each Play or seek starts a new Sampler "session", and the old one is faded out and thrown away, because Tone's `releaseAll()` can't cancel notes that already have a release scheduled. Queues notes 0.25 s ahead. The head start is `max(50 ms, 3 × baseLatency)` so it works on slower devices. `press`/`release` play notes by hand (mouse now, MIDI keyboard later) on a separate live Sampler that seeks don't cut off. `schedule()` takes an `until` so Learn mode never queues past the next chord I have to play, and scales its look-ahead and note lengths by the clock's speed. Everything plays through one master gain (`setVolume`, squared slider value; it starts at 50%, `DEFAULT_VOLUME`), and `setSustain` is the pedal.
- `src/keyboard.ts`: positions of the keys (`layoutKeys(width, low, high)`, all 88 by default), `fitRange()` (the octaves a song uses, widened to a C up to a B and to at least two octaves, for "Fit piano to .midi"), the keyboard strip's layout, `keyAt()` (which key is under a click), and `keyLabel()` (the name shown on a key: C, C#, D ..., with the octave on every C). `src/renderer.ts`: draws a frame onto a see-through canvas. Notes are colored per hand from `src/colors.ts` (`setHandColors`; darker shade on black keys), and a lit key takes the color of the note on it. White keys are drawn 1 px in from each side, the same as the notes. A 4 px black rail sits above the keys, the black keys reach up to it, and notes land on it. It draws Free play's rising notes (`Trail`), a beam and a soft bloom where the note lands for lit keys (switch: `setKeyGlow`; both are cached sprites stretched to the key, `beamSprite` and `glowSprite`, so no gradient is made per key per frame; on a light background they are laid over the page instead of added to it), a glow along the rail in the theme color (`setRailGlow`), and key names (`setKeyNames`). It only loops over the notes that can be on screen (binary search; the notes are sorted by time). The glow along the rail grows while the sustain pedal is held (`setSustainGlow`), and on a light background the outlines and guide lines are dark (`setLightBackground`).
- `src/particles.ts`: the particle numbers, with no browser code. Nine choices (`PARTICLE_STYLES`: Off, Sparks, Embers, Fireflies, Stars, Bubbles, Confetti, Stardust, Streaks; Wisps and Lightning were taken out, and a saved one of those reads as Embers), each with its own speed, spread, gravity, drag, sway, jitter, life and size. Every style drifts a little and is drawn with a soft glow and a flicker or twinkle, like Embers and Fireflies. Embers cool from white-hot through their own color (the note's, or the one picked for the hand) to a dark version of it (`coolColor`, in 16 steps when drawn so the glow images are reused). Streaks keep a trail, one point every `TRAIL_GAP` (0.025 s) so it is as long at any frame rate. Stardust and Streaks also light a short flare on the key. `column` ("Rise in a column", on by default, `DEFAULT_COLUMN`) keeps 12% of the sideways movement, starts them within 35% of the key's width, and keeps 30% of a downward pull so Sparks, Stars and Confetti fly further up; each particle keeps the setting it was born with. `rise` (the Height slider, 50% to 300%, default 150%, `clampRise`) multiplies speed and life by its square root, so the distance grows with it. `Particles.burst` (a key lit up), `trickle` (a key held by hand, a steady rate at any frame rate), `release`, `step(dt)` and `clear`. At most `MAX_PARTICLES` (4000) at once; past it the oldest go (`makeRoom`), so a new note always gets its burst. `step(dt, top)` also drops a particle as soon as it floats above `top` (the renderer passes -40 px), and nearly invisible ones (alpha under 0.02) are not drawn. `clampIntensity` keeps the intensity in 25% to 200%, `parseStyle` reads the saved style, `fadeOf` is how see-through one is near its end. `renderer.ts` (`setParticles`) bursts once for every note that started since the last frame (so fast repeated notes on a lit key, and notes shorter than a frame, each get their own; a seek or a jump back sets off none) and once for each key newly pressed by hand, in its note's color, or the color picked for that hand (`setParticleColors`; Confetti: every bright color), trickles keys held by hand only, steps the old ones by the real time between frames (at most 0.05 s) before adding the new ones, so a new particle is drawn exactly where it is born: the middle of the black rail. They are drawn last in the frame (over the key flash and the bar glow), clipped at the top of the keys, adding light on a dark page. Glows are one cached soft sprite per color, drawn with `drawImage`. Tested by `scripts/test-particles.mjs`.
- `src/score.ts`: the Learn score, with no browser code: `Score` keeps `hits`, `misses`, `combo` (hits in a row, a miss resets it to 0), `best`, and `percent` (whole percent right, **100% when nothing has been played**, so a wrong first note is 0 of 1 = 0%). Tested in `scripts/test-learn.mjs`.
- `src/limit.ts`: the limited octave range for Learn, with no browser code. `clampLimit` keeps two borders on white keys, low no higher than high (defaults C4 = 60 and C6 = 84), `outsideLimit`, `splitByLimit` (notes inside and outside), `snapLow`/`snapHigh` (drag to the nearest white key edge), `borderX` (where a border is drawn; at the piano's edge if its key isn't shown), and `AutoCredit`, which counts the notes the song plays for me as the song moves strictly past each one (so Learn stopped exactly on a chord doesn't count a note that hasn't sounded). `LIMIT_STYLES`/`parseLimitStyle` are the five bar looks (below), `LIMIT_COVERS`/`parseLimitCover` are Whole screen or Piano only, and `clampLimitShade` keeps the shade strength in 0.1 to 0.9. `renderer.ts` (`setRangeBorders(range, style, keysOnly, shade)`) shades what is outside and draws the two bars in the chosen style. Tested by `scripts/test-limit.mjs`.
- `src/hands.ts`: `orderTracksByPitch()` renumbers tracks by average pitch, highest first, so track 0 is the right hand (a guess from pitch; checked on my two songs, where the tracks never cross). It also returns `order`, which file track each ranked track came from, for the Tracks popup. Tested in `scripts/test-learn.mjs`.
- `src/learn.ts`: Learn mode logic with no browser code. `nextTime()` is the next chord I have to play. `groupChords()` turns notes into chords (within 50 ms), and `LearnSession.update(songTime, pressTimes)` says "run" or "wait at this chord until these keys are played". `early` is how early a key may be pressed (default `EARLY` 0.1 s, `parseEarly` reads the saved value), and `oneByOne` makes a chord go through once each note has been pressed at least once. `judge(midi, time)` rules on each key press for the score: 'hit' (a note of the chord being played, no earlier than `early` before it), 'miss' (a key that is not a note of that chord), or 'extra' (ignored by the score: a right key pressed too early, which is not punished but only counts when pressed again inside the early window; a right key pressed again before the chord is finished; any key after the last chord). `main.ts` stops and restarts the clock from it. Tested by `scripts/test-learn.mjs`.
- `src/colors.ts`: the fixed list of eighteen note colors (red, coral, orange, amber, yellow, lime, green, mint, teal, sky, dark blue, violet, magenta, rose, white, silver, charcoal, black; Orange, Green, Magenta and Silver were added 2026-10-16, and every dark shade but Amber's and Sky's follows the lightness-0.2, saturation-x-0.65 rule, which `scripts/test-colors.mjs` checks), each with a main shade and a darker shade for black keys. Charcoal and black are `noTheme`: they are left out of the Theme row (`THEME_COLORS`). No custom colors. Defaults: amber (right hand), sky (left hand), amber (theme). `parseSavedColors` reads what the browser saved, `dimmed()` makes the left hand dimmer. It also holds the eighteen backgrounds (`BACKGROUNDS`: black, charcoal #121212, slate, espresso, eleven deep colors including indigo, and three light ones, white, paper and mist, which are `light`), `parseSavedBackground`, and `surfaceRgb` (the header's color over a background). Tested by `scripts/test-colors.mjs`.
- `src/history.ts`: the songs I opened from my own files, kept in the browser (IndexedDB), newest first, at most 30, none over 2 MB. `rememberSong`, `forgetSong`, `clearHistory`, `listHistory`. The ordering helper is tested by `scripts/test-history.mjs`.
- `src/folder.ts`: my own folder of songs, straight from disk (File System Access API, Chrome and Edge only; `folderSupported()` says whether to show it). `chooseFolder`, `canRead` (may ask the browser for permission, which needs a click), `listSongs` (the `.mid` and `.midi` files directly in the folder, sorted, no subfolders; hidden `._` files skipped), `readSong`, and the handle kept in its own IndexedDB (`rememberFolder`, `recallFolder`, `forgetFolder`). TypeScript's built-in types lack `showDirectoryPicker` and the permission calls, so small shapes are declared there. `isMidiName` and `sortSongs` are tested by `scripts/test-folder.mjs`.
- `src/tempo.ts`: tempo helpers with no browser code. `tempoAt()` is the tempo at a song time, `formatBpm()` rounds the library's floats for display, `clampBpm()`/`metronomeTempo()` keep the metronome between 20 and 240 BPM (a typed tempo, else the song's, else 100), and `buildBeats()`/`firstBeatIndex()` turn the file's tempo and time signature events into every beat of the song and find the next beat after a skip. Tested by `scripts/test-tempo.mjs`.
- `src/metronome.ts`: the metronome (Tone.js has none). A short synth click queued ahead on the audio clock, like the piano. **Free** mode runs by itself at the tempo it is given, from the moment it is turned on. **Song** mode follows the song's beats, only while the song plays, and `resync()` re-aligns it after a Play, pause, skip, Restart or speed change. Its clicks go through the piano's master volume.
- `src/template.ts`: the page's HTML and the inline SVG icons (drawn by me, no icon library).
- `src/tooltip.ts`: the site's own tooltips. Any element with a `title` gets one: the first hover moves the title to `data-tip` and shows it in a styled popover (top layer, so it shows above popups). An empty title means no tooltip. Not shown for touch.
- `src/smooth-scroll.ts`: eases mouse-wheel scrolling in the page's own scroll areas (Settings, popups, song lists, the welcome card). Trackpads and the reduced-motion setting keep the browser's scrolling.
- `src/computer-keys.ts`: the computer keyboard as a piano. `noteForKey(code, octave)` maps a key position (e.g. `KeyZ` is C4, `KeyQ` is C5) to a piano key, shifted by octaves and dropped if it falls off the 88 keys. The up and down arrow keys change the octave in `main.ts`. `keyCapForNote(midi, octave)` goes the other way (the key's text on a US keyboard, the top row where two rows reach), for the "Computer keys" key names in Settings (Note names | Computer keys, shown when key names are on; it replaces the note names on the keys and notes, follows the arrow keys' octave, and hides "Only label the C keys"). Tested by `scripts/test-keys.mjs`.
- `src/midi-input.ts`: Web MIDI. Asks for access, listens to every connected keyboard (and re-checks when one is plugged in), and turns note on and note off into `noteOn(midi, velocity 0 to 1)` and `noteOff(midi)` calls. Passes the sustain pedal (controller 64, down at a value of 64 or more) as `sustain(down)`. Calls `warning(text)` when MIDI can't be used at all (not supported, blocked, failed) and `warning(null)` when it can. Ignores everything else. `main.ts` connects these to `piano.press`/`release` and lights the keys.
- `src/main.ts`: the welcome card (the site title, three mode buttons in the theme color, a pill that shows whether a MIDI keyboard was found, a list of past songs and sample songs, and a dashed click-or-drop box; no mode picked at first), drop-a-.mid-file-anywhere, the computer keyboard handlers, the Autoplay / Free play / Learn tabs, the song popup (drag a file onto the dashed box or click to browse, "Your songs" history, a "Sample songs" list from `samplesongs/`, and "Your folder"), the Settings panel (see below), file loading (`parseSong` reads a file into a `Song`; skips drum tracks, empty tracks, and notes outside the 88 keys; tags each note with its track, ranked by pitch), separate songs for Autoplay and Learn (`modeSongs`: each keeps its own tracks, hand and place), the hand picker, the NOW PLAYING pill, the Tracks popup (the switched-off tracks are filtered out of `notes`), the floating control bar (draggable by its grip; snaps to the four edges, the corners, and the middle of each edge; its place is saved in the browser), the draggable Waiting display (two fixed snap spots), the glass header, the speed button, the volume slider with a typed percentage and a mute button, the metronome button with its tempo box and Free/Song chip, the BPM chip in the header, the sustain pedal (space bar), and the frame loop. Settings holds: note colors (left | right, swap, dim the left hand), a theme color, a background color (preview tiles), Learn timing, key names, and the options (background glow, bar glow, key glow, sustain glow, hiding the tempo, notes count, MIDI keyboard and Sustain chips, show Waiting for, play chords note by note, fill an empty mode with the other mode's song, the computer keyboard switch), the keyboard layout, the credits, and a hold-to-reset-all button at the bottom. Everything in Settings is remembered in the browser.

## Decided behavior
- Plain playback keeps playing when the tab is hidden. Learn mode must pause when the tab is hidden. Maybe a setting later.
- While the tab is hidden, Autoplay keeps playing: Tone's timer (it runs on a Web Worker, which the browser doesn't slow) queues the next second of notes and metronome clicks every 0.1 s, since the frame loop stops in a hidden tab. When the tab comes back nothing is reset unless the audio had stopped (resetting cuts off ringing notes). The frame loop always asks for the next frame, even if one frame throws, and the canvas skips drawing while it is a sliver (resizing the window or opening the inspector).
- "Play chords note by note" is on by default.
- The sustain pedal marks inside a song file are ignored. The pedal (space bar or a MIDI pedal) only holds the notes I play by hand. Drum tracks and notes outside the 88 keys are skipped.
- The control bar snap distances are 10 px to an edge, 15 px either side of the middle of an edge, and 20 px for a corner (`SNAP_DISTANCE`, `CENTER_DISTANCE`, `CORNER_DISTANCE` in `main.ts`). A row in a corner stays a row at the top or bottom, and a column stays a column at the side.
- In the Tracks popup, at least one track has to stay on. The hand picker only shows with exactly two tracks on, and it goes back to Both when a track is switched. The Tracks button is there in Autoplay and Learn, not Free play.
- Learn's `EARLY` window stays in song seconds, so playback speed doesn't change how far early a key can be on screen.
- Autoplay and Learn each keep their own song, with their own tracks, hand and place. A mode with no song yet gets a fresh copy of the other mode's song when it is opened, if the Settings switch allows. After that they never affect each other. A file dropped on the welcome screen or in Free play waits for me to pick a mode, and is dropped if I close that popup without picking.
- The song popup's "Your songs" list is the history of files I opened from my own device (kept in the browser, newest first, reopening one moves it to the top). Sample songs come from `samplesongs/`, which is committed, and are listed most notes first (ties by name) in the song popup and on the welcome screen: `sortSamplesByNotes()` in `main.ts` reads every sample once at start, counts its notes the way `parseSong` does (no drum tracks, nothing off the 88 keys), and redraws both lists, so for a moment they are in name order. `songs/` is gitignored and is not read by the page.
- "Your folder" (song popup and welcome screen, Chrome and Edge only) lists the `.mid` files directly in a folder I chose. It is not added to the history, since the files stay on disk. The browser remembers the folder but asks for permission again on a new visit, so an "Allow access" button shows until it is given. The welcome tab is always shown where the browser has the feature, with a "Choose a folder" button inside, because nothing else on the welcome screen could choose one. Other browsers don't show it.
- The theme color is the interface's accent everywhere (tabs and their highlight, pills, hover and focus colors, switches, the snap guides, the volume and BPM boxes, the play button, the sliders, the welcome buttons) and the color of the glow behind the notes. The glow has its own switch. The small logo mark stays orange. The thin line of light on the welcome card stays on even when the background glow is off.
- The metronome starts in Free mode, which runs by itself from the moment it is turned on, in every mode including Free play. Song mode follows the song's beats and needs a song in Autoplay or Learn (the choice is greyed out otherwise). In Song mode the tempo box is read-only. A typed tempo (20 to 240 BPM, real beats per minute, not scaled by the playback speed) stays across songs until Auto is pressed. The accent comes every N beats, N being the top number of the song's time signature (4 with none).
- The Learn score (dock, Learn only): a combo counter, a percentage, and the hits and misses (a green check and a red cross). Each key press is judged once by `LearnSession.judge`, from `noteDown` in `main.ts`, only while Learn is running or waiting; Learn is first brought up to date with the earlier presses, so a quick run of keys is judged against the right chord. Pressing the next correct note too early is neutral (no miss, no hit, combo kept); the chord still only goes through with a press inside the Learn timing window. A hit adds to the combo, a miss resets it. The percentage is hits / (hits + misses), 100% before anything is played. Restart, and a new song, tracks or hand, give a clean score; pausing and seeking do not. "Hits and misses" in Settings (on by default, saved, covered by reset-all) hides only the counters (the page class `hide-counts`). The combo number takes the theme color from 5 in a row and pops on each hit. Sideways, the score stacks; on phones (up to 600 px) it shares the bottom row with the seek slider, with the combo stacked as a number over COMBO, so the dock stays two rows. Checked in headless Chrome with a five-note song and typed keys, at 1280, 390 and 320 px wide. Not checked on a real phone or with a MIDI keyboard.
- "Limited octave range" (Settings, off by default, saved, covered by reset-all) is for a MIDI keyboard with fewer keys than the song needs. In Learn only, two bars sit on the piano (default C4 and C6), dragged above the keys (not on them, so no key press is stolen; 9 px for a mouse, 18 px for a finger; the cursor turns to a resize arrow), and snap to white keys. The practiced hand's notes outside the bars are played by the song (they join `soundNotes`), Learn never waits for them, and each counts as a hit when the song moves past it (`credit` in `main.ts`), so it adds to the combo and the percentage. A key I press outside the bars is neither a hit nor a miss. Letting go of a bar, or switching the option, reloads Learn through `applyHand(true)`, and the score is kept (the `keepScore` flag; a new song, tracks or hand still start clean). While the option is on, a "Bar style" row appears under it with five looks (`options.limitStyle`, saved, default Bar): Bar (the full-height line with a grip), Line (thin line), Dashed (thin dashed line), Marker (a small arrow and a short line just above the keys, nothing higher), and Shade (no bar, only a slightly stronger shading). Under the style row: "Covers" (Whole screen, or Piano only, `options.limitCover`: the bars and shading then start at the rail and cover only the keys, so the falling notes stay clear, and a bar is grabbed within 60 px above the keys instead of anywhere above them), a "Shade the outside" switch (`options.limitShade`, on by default; off shows only the bars), and a Shade slider (10% to 90%, default 45%, `options.limitShadeOpacity`, `clampLimitShade`; hidden while the shade is off). With Shade style and the shade off, nothing shows at all, by choice. The bars are grabbed the same way in every style. Both hands chosen and the option on: the song is no longer silent, it plays the notes outside the bars. Checked in headless Chrome at 1280 x 800 with a four-note song (C3, E4, C7, E4): the song didn't stop at C3 or C7, each counted as a hit (4 hits, 0 misses, 100%); dragging the high bar from C6 to E5 while the song waited kept the score (3 hits, combo 3) and saved 76; switching the option off kept it too; all five styles were clicked, saved and looked at. Cover, shade switch and shade strength checked by reading canvas pixels and screenshots (above the keys the shade is gone with Piano only; a black key reads 23 with the shade off, 18 at 20%, 13 at 45%, 2 at 90%), and in Piano only a drag far above the keys grabs nothing while one just above them moves the bar. Not checked on a real phone, with a MIDI keyboard, or with two hands.
